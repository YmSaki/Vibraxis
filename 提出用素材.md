# Vibraxis — your set, your words

**Vibraxis is an AI club DJ that turns a crate of tracks and a sentence of intent
into a continuously mixed, beat-matched set.** Your music app can shuffle, but it
can't DJ. Playlists are static; shuffle ignores tempo, key, and the energy in the
room. Vibraxis is a *resident DJ*: give it music and it selects, beat-matches, and
crossfades on its own — endlessly — while you steer it in plain language.

## Inspiration

I love music. I write my own, I listen to my DJ friends blend one track into the
next, I catch songs on the radio, and I keep music playing while I work. But when I
start my usual playlist, I sometimes just want something fresh — and shuffle can't
read the room. A real DJ reads the moment: they hold the energy, choose a key- and
tempo-compatible record, and blend it in at exactly the right time. We wanted that as
software you can simply talk to.

## What it does

- **Runs an autonomous, endless set.** Load a crate, start it, and Vibraxis keeps
  choosing the next track and executing a **beat-matched crossfade** between two decks
  — with **no per-mix approval**. It does not stop to ask "shall I play this track?";
  a DJ never does.
- **You steer it in plain language.** "Something more energetic," "keep it moody, then
  slowly pick it up" — GPT-5.6 turns your words into a bounded *intent* that shapes
  what it plays next. A request is steering, not a command to interrupt the set.
- **It explains every move.** Each decision shows its provenance: the interpreted
  intent, why the track fits (tempo distance, Camelot key compatibility, energy
  direction), and which stage produced it.
- **The booth is always yours.** Grab the crossfader mid-mix and the automation yields
  instantly; hit PANIC and everything stops. The AI is the performer — never the
  owner.
- **Ships with fully licensed, pre-analyzed music.** A royalty-free library (BGMer),
  analyzed offline for BPM, beat/downbeat grids, key/Camelot, sections, and energy — a
  legal record box an AI is allowed to mix.

## How we built it

TypeScript everywhere, in an npm-workspaces monorepo (React + Vite frontend, a
dependency-free Node `node:http` backend, shared types + JSON Schemas). Two ideas
carry the project:

- **A real two-deck instrument, controlled by a protocol.** The audio runtime lives in
  the browser and is driven over a MessagePort contract we call **VDAP** — a revisioned
  canonical state store, an intent lifecycle, and a command dispatcher that reserves a
  beat-matched transition *atomically* at a bar boundary. The Web Audio engine has two
  decks, a 3-band EQ, DJ / equal-power crossfader curves, playback-rate limits, and a
  limiter so it can never clip.
- **A bounded, auditable AI pipeline** — we deliberately do **not** let an LLM drive the
  audio engine:

  ```
  natural language ─▶ GPT-5.6 ─▶ DjIntent (bounded JSON)
  DjIntent ─▶ deterministic engine ─▶ scored ranking + disclosed shortlist
  shortlist ─▶ Codex ─▶ DjDecision (track, deck, crossfade bars)
  DjDecision ─▶ schema + semantic + binding validation ─▶ beat-matched transition
  ```

  GPT-5.6 (via Structured Outputs) interprets intent; a pure, deterministic engine
  scores every candidate by BPM proximity, Camelot compatibility, and energy direction;
  Codex picks from a *disclosed* shortlist in a read-only, network-disabled sandbox.
  Every model output is Ajv- and semantically-validated and **rejected unchanged on any
  violation — never repaired or silently substituted** (our top collaboration rule).

- **Offline analysis you can trust.** A Python/librosa `analyze-tool` produces the
  catalog and per-track analysis. Because librosa's dynamic beat tracking wandered on
  constant-tempo DAW material, we built a **rigid beat-grid solver** and verified every
  grid **by ear** with a purpose-built audition tool, excluding tracks that failed the
  human gate.

- **An autonomous conductor** reuses the tested selection + transition primitives and
  adds the loop: it watches the active deck and, near the outro, auto-selects and
  beat-matches the next track — endlessly, with no approval. The canonical model
  (Planner/Executor state machine, request semantics) is specified in `docs/`.

- **Built protocol-first with Codex.** Codex wrote the VDAP specification **and a
  machine checker for it** before a single feature existed, then the analyzer, the audio
  engine, and the runtime — each stage designed, tested, and committed. At submission,
  `npm run check` (protocol docs, contract schemas, full type-checking, and the test
  suite) plus a deterministic end-to-end smoke run **green in GitHub Actions CI**.

## Challenges we ran into

- **Beat grids you can hear.** Getting downbeats right on constant-tempo tracks meant
  abandoning dynamic tracking for a rigid-grid solver, then auditioning every grid by
  ear with sample-accurate click overlays. Four tracks that couldn't be made musically
  correct were excluded rather than shipped wrong.
- **Making the AI safe *and* honest.** The exact model id is pinned and the response is
  rejected on mismatch — and we discovered live that `gpt-5.6` is a *family alias* that
  the API resolves to a concrete variant (e.g. `gpt-5.6-luna`). We made the model
  caller-selectable via an env var while keeping the exact-match guard, instead of
  quietly accepting whatever came back.
- **Autonomy vs. control.** The hardest reframing was conceptual: Vibraxis is not
  "AI proposes a mix, human clicks apply" — it is a resident DJ that runs on its own and
  takes your words as *future* steering. Technically, the tricky part is firing each mix
  early enough before a track's outro that it never ends mid-transition.

## Accomplishments that we're proud of

- **Autonomous, beat-matched mixing running live in the browser — with no per-mix
  approval.** You can watch it choose the next track and crossfade on its own.
- A **bounded, auditable AI pipeline**: natural language in, *validated* DJ decisions
  out, every step explained, nothing repaired behind your back.
- A real instrument with a **documented protocol (VDAP)**, human-verified beat grids,
  and a **green CI gate** — 389 backend/frontend/contract tests plus 33 analyzer tests.
- **Protocol-first development with Codex**: a spec and a checker before features, so the
  build stayed disciplined end to end.

## What we learned

- **Give an AI a strong contract and it builds like an engineer.** Writing the spec and
  a machine checker first made every later stage calmer and more correct.
- **The right division of labor.** Let the LLM interpret ambiguous human intent; let
  deterministic, testable logic make the safety-critical audio decisions. Don't hand the
  whole problem to the model.
- **"Autonomous" doesn't mean "no human."** The strongest model is autonomous *by
  default* with instant, total human override — steer with a sentence, grab the booth any
  time, and never be asked to approve a mix.

## What's next for Vibraxis

- **The full conductor**: a Planner/Executor state machine with two-deep look-ahead,
  pre-cued transitions, and structure-aware timing that never cuts a drop (spec already
  written in `docs/`).
- **Richer blends**: EQ-swap and channel-fader transitions — the other DJ "schools" —
  beyond the current crossfader mix.
- **Streaming catalogs**: the engine is catalog-agnostic by design; the same interface
  is meant to mount Audius/Jamendo now and partner catalogs later (research in `memo.md`).
- **Any agent at the decks**: the same port the built-in AI plays through is designed to
  mount an MCP server, so tomorrow another agent could sit at these decks.
